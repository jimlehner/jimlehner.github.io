About

Hello! I'm Jim Lehner, a manufacturing and process improvement engineer who specializes in reducing costs and improving the quality of manufactured products by leveraging industry's most valuable, yet underutilized, resource: data.

Data has become the currency of our time, yet few know what to do with it. As a result, much of what passes for analysis is little more than simple comparisons. This approach compares a current value with a past value, a target value, or a descriptive statistic. When the current value is not as "good" as it should be (whatever that means), explanations are required and reports must be written. A root cause must be identified and control plans must be put in place. Resources are dedicated to addressing what is assumed to be a problem, and improvement is expected to follow.

The trouble with this approach is that it is built on a false premise. In the mathematical plan, where there is no uncertainty, numbers that are not the same are always different. Here, 1 is always 1, and 2 is always 2. The rub is that we do not live in the mathemtical plane. We live in the real world. In the real world, when a manufacturer lays a two-micron film on a two-micron substrate, the result will rarely be four microns. The best we can hope for is an average thickness of four microns. This is because, in the real world, numbers that are different are NOT always evidence of difference. In fact, two numbers can be different and still represent the same thing! The failure to recognize this truth is what makes solving manufacturing problems seem so challenging, even though a tried-and-true solution is within reach.

Having worked in the aerospace, automotive, medical device, industrial machining, healthcare, and defense industries, I've been afforded the opportunity to solve a wide variety of business and manufacturing problems. Regardless of the product, project, or industry, my success has consistently revealed the same underlying truth: the key to managing the chaos of manufacturing is understanding variation, and the key to understanding variation lies in your process data. Turning that data into insights and those insights into actions that make reduce the costs and improve the quality of manufactured products is my specialty. Let's discuss how these skills can be put to work for you!

When not solving manufacturing problems, I spend my time writing about and teaching the methods that have been pivotal to my successful. Chief among these techniques are the process behavior chart and the way of thinking know as Statistical Process Control (SPC). If you are interested in learning more about my methods or have a group that you think would benefit from discussing them, message me at James.Lehner@gmail.com (or via the contact page). Additional information can be found at The Math Matters and BrokenQuality.com.
