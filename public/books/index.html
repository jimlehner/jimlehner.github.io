The Virus of Variation

Making Sense of Death and Data Using Process Behavior Charts

Using the story of the 19th-century Hungarian physician, Ignaz Semmelweis, as a guide, The Virus of Variation teaches readers how to make sense of data using process behavior charts. Also known as control charts, process behavior charts are the only tool capable of making the invisible world of variation visible, allowing us to anticipate the future based on the past. This knowledge helps individuals and teams stop being reactive and start being proactive. It replaces luck with a math- and science-based methodology that reduces costs, improves quality, and, at least for Semmelweis, saves the lives of people in need.

Some of the topics covered include:

  • How to build a process behavior chart (control chart)
  • How to characterize process behavior and take actions that facilitate improvement
  • The right (and wrong) way to calculate process limits
  • The principles of process improvement
  • Much more!

How to Build an XmR Chart (with Excel)

Special software is NOT required

It is often assumed that the process behavior chart can only constructed using special software like Minitab or JMP. This could not be further from the truth. Excel is more than capable of this task. As proof of this point, How to Build an XmR Chart with Excel, walks you through the six step process of constructing an XmR chart in Excel. Along the way you will learn how to:

  • Import data using Power Query
  • Leverage useful table behaviors like automatic table expansion and formula filling
  • Apply conditional formatting to tables
  • Characterize process behavior as predictable or unpredictable
  • Discriminate between the two types of variation

Understanding the Process Capability Indices

Terms, interpreation, & context

The process capability indices are a standard, albeit misunderstood, method by which engineers quantify and grade process performance. Unfortunately, the terms that compose the indices and how the indices are calculated are not widely understood. The Broken Quality Initiative is on a mission to change that with Understanding the Process Capability Indices. After reading this document and completing its exercises, readers will be able to:

  • Calculate the terms that compose the process capability indices
  • Understand the difference between Sigma(X) and the standard deviation (s)
  • Calculate the process capability indices (Cp, Cpk, Pp, and Ppk)
  • Explain the differences between the capability ratios (Cp and Cpk) and the performance ratios (Pp and Ppk)
  • Interpret the process capability indices as standalone values and through comparison with each other
  • Understand why the indices should ALWAYS be presented in the context of a process behavior chart and capability histogram
